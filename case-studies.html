Four Phases, No Guesswork

The same four phases on every job, whether it is an app, a server room, or a branch network. Each one ends with paperwork you approve and keep.

Why One Method for Everything

What You Approve Is What Gets Built

Technology projects tend to fail in one of two ways: the plan was based on guesses, or nobody recorded what was actually delivered. Working the same way every time protects against both.

Phase 1

Survey

We walk the site, list every server, device, and application involved, and measure how they perform today. You get: a findings report that lists risks plainly.

Phase 1

Requirements

We talk to the people who use the systems and the people who pay for them: what must not stop, how much growth is expected, and what the budget can carry.

Phase 2

Blueprint

Drawings, specifications, sizing, and a parts list. You get: a design document and a fixed quotation.

Phase 2

Cut-Over Plan

A dated plan for installation and switch-over, with agreed maintenance windows and a written way back if something fails.

Phase 3

Build

Our own engineers install, configure, code, and migrate, sticking to the design you signed.

Phase 3

Acceptance Test

Your team watches while we test every item in the specification. You get: a signed test report and as-built records.

Phase 4

Run

Planned visits, monitoring, and help when you need it, under a support agreement. You get: regular health reports.

Our Rule

Check first. Prove it works. Write it all down.
